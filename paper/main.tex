\title{
An Auditable Complexity-Aware PAC-Bayesian Framework for
Linear and Takagi--Sugeno Time-Series Forecasting under
Temporal Dependence
}
		
		
		\author[su]{Muneef Abdulkareem Farea Ahmed\corref{cor1}}
		
		\ead{muneef.ahmed@su.edu.ye}
		
		\ead[url]{https://orcid.org/0009-0000-3974-6414}
		
		\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
		
		
		\affiliation[su]{
			organization={Faculty of Computer and Information Technology,
				Sana'a University},
			city={Sana'a},
			country={Yemen}
		}
		
		
		
\begin{abstract}
PAC-Bayesian certificates can support deployment decisions for time-series
forecasters, but their operational value depends on separating the prediction
object covered by the certificate from the forecast actually evaluated. This
paper presents an auditable framework for linear and first-order
Takagi--Sugeno (TSK) forecasting under overlapping and potentially
nonstationary observations. Chronological prior construction and an explicit
hierarchical code account for model family, lag, regularization, radius, rule
count, localization, and prior scale. The original frozen experiments use
deterministic posterior-center forecasts and treat the certificate as one
screening signal. A strictly retrospective alignment audit additionally
computes the exact clipped Gaussian Bayesian model average (BMA) and the exact
expected clipped squared loss while preserving frozen candidate identities,
the original PJM gate thresholds, and exclusion of test outcomes from decisions; for Tetouan, the PJM gate is transferred only as a post-hoc diagnostic.

Experiments on 30 synthetic series show that removing complete TSK rule blocks
reduces dimension, Gaussian KL divergence, and certificate value relative to a
dense 12-rule model, although small fixed rule bases can match radius control.
The original Tetouan gate admits two models that fail on the held-out horizon,
whereas the internally locked PJM gate selects four simple linear or affine
predictors that outperform the seasonal fallback. In the retrospective
exact-BMA audit, both Tetouan selections fail the stricter gate and all four
frozen PJM selections pass; this audit was added after outcomes were opened and
is not a new prospective or internally locked experiment. Secondary post-hoc SARIMA and ETS models
remain competitive. The evidence therefore supports auditable complexity
accounting, prediction-object alignment, conservative screening, and
abstention rather than a general claim of TSK superiority.
\end{abstract}
